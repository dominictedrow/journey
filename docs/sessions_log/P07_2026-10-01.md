### Learning notes
- **Why fusing elementwise ops matters.** On a GPU, `y = x + γ·branch` written as three ops reads and writes the whole
  token tensor three times; GEMMs are compute-bound but these ops are bound by memory bandwidth. `addcmul` does the
  same maths in one read/write pass. `torch.compile` does this automatically; in eager mode you do it by hand.
- **Memory format.** A tensor's shape [N, C, H, W] says nothing about its order in memory. `channels_last` stores the
  channels of one pixel next to each other (NHWC). LayerNorm over channels then reads contiguous memory, and
  `permute` becomes free (a view) instead of a copy.
- **Selective activation checkpointing.** Checkpointing a block frees its saved activations and recomputes them in
  backward. The best blocks to checkpoint have many activations per FLOP: early, high-resolution conv stages. Late
  transformer stages are the opposite (lots of compute per byte), so recomputing them is expensive.
- **Why a residual stream may be fp32 under autocast.** A parameter kept in fp32 (LayerScale γ) promotes what it
  multiplies. That's a silent dtype change, worth checking with a one-line test whenever you mix fp32 params into
  fp16 activations.
